Chapter 2 · The Counterintuitive Trap

Why Mixing Vendors on Every Prompt Backfires

Intuition says: "Route every prompt to whichever company is cheapest." That intuition is wrong for interactive coding.

93%–97% of your input tokens are warm prompt cache reads (the glowing blue ring). Watch the amber cross-vendor bolt shatter the ring: bouncing a routine turn from Claude Code to Codex or Grok mid-task forces the second vendor to re-read your repo cold (5x–10x token penalty + 20s startup lag).

Chapter 3 · Two Completely Different Worlds

Corporations Buy By the Drink. Developers Prepay.

Most AI proxies are built for corporations that don't have Max/Pro/Ultra seats—they pay per million tokens on every call. If a developer routes personal coding through a paid cloud proxy, they pay twice.

World 1 (Green Left — Your Machine): Strip all API keys. Keep paid cloud proxies OFF. Optimize your prepaid seats locally at $0 extra cost.
World 2 (Cyan Right — Apps You Ship): Production apps do buy by the drink. Never hardcode a model ID—call 5 tier aliases (fast | medium | substantial | hard | exceptional) backed by a swappable router.

Chapter 4 · Loosening the Pipes

5-Tier Local Routing + 4 Quota-Hygiene Rules

Watch the red wall dissolve into 5 balanced tiers with a 50% /compact relief ring venting bloat before it hits your cache:

• Fast (32%): Bulk scans & lookups isolated in Haiku / Neon Low / Luna subagents.
• Medium (54%): Routine edits & tests directly on your warm coordinator cache.
• Substantial (7%): Multi-file features on Opus 5.5 Med / Neon High / Sol High.
• Hard (5%) & Exceptional (<2%): Pre-distilled context sent surgically to Neon High / Opus High / Astra High / Fable 5.2 High—never open-ended discovery.

Myth: "Bounce every prompt to the cheapest vendor."
Reality: Stay in-vendor for 90% of turns to keep your 95%+ warm cache. Only cross vendors for adversarial second opinions or cold heavy multi-file offloads.
Myth: "Turn thinking up to xhigh/max for best quality."

Myth: "1M context means I can keep one chat open all day."
Reality: Past 50% context, every reply re-processes 500k+ tokens and triggers massive cache-write fees. Compacting at 50% + isolating searches in fast subagents saves 70%+.

Evolution of Thought · Don't Accept Recipes Blindly

4 Painful Lessons That Forced This Architecture

Every rule in the Starter Kit exists because a simpler idea failed in production. Here is the forensic trail so you can verify our logic against your own machine.

Lesson 01 · The $1,600 Wake-Up

Personal Seats Must Strip All API Keys

Leaving ANTHROPIC_API_KEY or OPENROUTER_API_KEY in your shell turns prepaid coding tools into metered corporate billing. Our router enforces subscriptionOnlyEnv() and keeps cloud proxies OFF locally.

Lesson 02 · Why TUI Wrappers Failed

Native Hooks Beat Process Wrappers

Wrapping interactive CLIs broke warm prompt caches, TTY resize, and streaming UX. A <50ms native prompt hook keeps your warm session intact and delegates only searches or escalations to subagents.

Lesson 03 · The "Advice-Only" Illusion

Dashboards Can Lie While Burn Stays High

For 5 weeks, logs showed green "routed" ticks while actual Claude transcripts stayed 57% Opus—because hooks only suggested models without changing session defaults or compacting at 50%. Now we audit real transcripts.

Lesson 04 · The 212M-Token Blowup

Never Send Frontier Models Exploring

In KingBench 4, letting Fable 5.2 roam raw repos burned 212M tokens. Scouting with Fast/Medium first and handing Frontier a distilled 3-file brief yields higher accuracy at 1/10th the quota.
